Bimanual ICRT Architecture

How a next-token-prediction model built for one Franka arm was retargeted onto a two-armed, three-camera robot — and where the learning actually happens.

Figure 1 — forward pass

Every timestep becomes two tokens

ICRT treats manipulation as sequence modelling. The whole design rests on compressing one moment — three camera views plus joint positions — into a single state token, so a 512-step context stays tractable at 15 Hz control.

Figure 2 — why it is "in-context"

The loss only starts after a demonstration ends

Nothing in Figure 1 makes the model learn from a prompt. That comes from how the sequence is cut. Several complete demonstrations are concatenated; a boundary is picked at random; loss is computed only on what follows it. The model is forced to use the earlier episodes, because they are the only thing distinguishing one task from another.

Measured consequence. On 280–412 frame episodes, a sequence length of 32 leaves only 10% of windows containing a boundary — so loss reads 0.0000 on roughly nine iterations in ten. Training completes and reports success while learning nothing. At 512 the figure is 95%, and loss falls from 0.3550 to 0.2399 in one epoch.

Each arm contributes [xyz(3), rot_6d(6), gripper(1)], left block then right. Head, torso lift and the mobile base are recorded but never commanded — which is what keeps the Cartesian space invertible, since an end-effector pose in base_link would otherwise be satisfiable by raising the torso instead of moving the arm.
